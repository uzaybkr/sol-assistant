A few years ago, a cyber-insurance application was a page of checkboxes. Today it is a detailed security questionnaire, and a "no" on the wrong question can mean a higher premium, an exclusion, or no offer at all.

Every insurer writes its own questions, but the same controls come up again and again. If you can answer "yes" to the ones below, and show evidence, your renewal gets much easier.

## 1. Multi-factor authentication (MFA) everywhere that matters

This is the question that decides most applications. Insurers typically want MFA on:

- **Email**, for every user
- **Remote access**, such as VPNs and remote desktop
- **Administrator accounts** and cloud management consoles

Stolen passwords are the most common way attackers get in. MFA stops most of those attempts even when the password is already in a criminal's hands.

## 2. Backups that ransomware can't reach

"Do you have backups?" is no longer enough. Expect questions like:

- Is at least one copy **offline, immutable, or separated** from your main network?
- Are backups **encrypted**?
- When did you last **test a restore**, and did it work?

Ransomware groups now look for backups first and delete or encrypt them before they strike. A backup that has never been restored is a hope, not a plan.

## 3. Endpoint detection and response (EDR)

Traditional antivirus looks for known bad files. EDR watches behavior, such as a program suddenly encrypting thousands of files, and can stop an attack in progress. Many insurers now ask for EDR on every workstation and server, and some ask whether someone is **monitoring its alerts around the clock**.

## 4. No remote desktop open to the internet

Remote desktop (RDP) exposed directly to the internet is one of the most common entry points for ransomware. Insurers ask about it directly, and some scan applicants from the outside to check. Remote access should go through a VPN or a secure gateway, with MFA.

## 5. Security awareness training

Most successful attacks start with a person: a convincing invoice, a fake login page, a "quick favor" from the boss. Insurers usually ask whether **every employee is trained at least once a year**, and increasingly whether you run **phishing simulations**.

## 6. Patching and admin rights

Two quieter questions that matter:

- Are **critical updates installed within a set window**, often 30 days?
- Do staff use **standard accounts** for daily work, with administrator rights limited to a few people?

## 7. A written incident response plan

When something happens, the first hours matter most. Insurers want to know that you have written down who to call, how to isolate affected systems, and how to reach your insurer's breach hotline.

## How to prepare for your renewal

1. **Take an honest inventory.** Our free [readiness check](/readiness) walks through 15 of the most common questions in about three minutes.
2. **Fix the critical gaps first.** MFA, offline backups, EDR and closing remote desktop usually have the biggest effect.
3. **Collect evidence.** Screenshots of MFA settings, a backup restore log, and training completion records make the application faster and more credible.
4. **Answer truthfully.** Misstating controls on an application can put a future claim at risk.
