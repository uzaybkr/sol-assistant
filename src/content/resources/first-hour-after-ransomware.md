A screen full of locked files and a ransom note is one of the worst moments a business owner can have. What you do in the first hour decides how bad the next week will be.

**Print this page. Keep it somewhere you can find it even if your computers are down.**

## Minute 0 to 10: contain it

- [ ] **Disconnect the affected computer from the network.** Unplug the network cable and turn off Wi-Fi.
- [ ] **Do not turn it off.** Memory can hold evidence that helps investigators, and some recovery options depend on it. Just disconnect it.
- [ ] **Check other computers** for the same ransom note or strangely renamed files, and disconnect any that are affected.
- [ ] **Disconnect backup drives** that are still plugged in, so they don't get encrypted too.

## Minute 10 to 30: call for help

- [ ] **Call your security provider** or IT support. If you're a Acme Cyber client, email us with URGENT in the subject line and a phone number.
- [ ] **Call your cyber-insurance hotline** if you have a policy. Many policies require prompt notice and have preferred response firms.
- [ ] **Write down what you saw**: the time, which machine, the ransom note text (take a photo with your phone), and anything unusual before it happened.

## Minute 30 to 60: protect the rest

- [ ] **Change passwords from a clean device**, starting with email and administrator accounts, and make sure multi-factor authentication is on.
- [ ] **Tell staff what's happening** and what they should and should not do. Rumors cause mistakes.
- [ ] **Don't contact the attackers** and **don't pay** before talking to your security provider, insurer and, if needed, an attorney. Paying does not guarantee your data back.

## Things not to do

- **Don't wipe or reinstall** anything yet. It destroys evidence you may need for insurance and investigation.
- **Don't restore from backup** until the attacker's access has been removed. Otherwise the restored systems can be encrypted again.
- **Don't try free "decryptor" downloads** from unknown sites. Some are malware.

## After the first hour

Your security team will work out how the attacker got in, remove their access, and restore systems from clean backups. You may have legal obligations to notify customers, patients or regulators, depending on your industry and the data involved, so involve your attorney early.

## The best time to prepare is before

- **Backups** that ransomware can't reach, tested with a real restore.
- **24/7 monitoring**, so an attack is spotted and stopped before it spreads.
- **Staff training**, because most ransomware starts with one email.
