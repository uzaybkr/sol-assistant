If your firm prepares tax returns or offers financial services to consumers, the Federal Trade Commission's Safeguards Rule almost certainly applies to you, no matter how small you are. The Rule was significantly strengthened in 2023, and many small firms have not caught up.

Here is what it asks for, translated into plain language.

## The core idea: a written information security program

The Rule requires a **written information security program** that protects customer information. The IRS points tax professionals in the same direction and publishes guidance on building a **written information security plan (WISP)**.

The program isn't a single document you file away. It is a set of decisions and safeguards you put in place, write down, and keep up to date.

## The checklist

**People and oversight**

- [ ] Name a **qualified individual** to oversee the program. This can be someone at your firm or an outside provider, but your firm stays responsible.
- [ ] Have that person **report to your leadership** at least once a year on the program and its risks.
- [ ] **Train your staff** on security, and keep the training current.

**Know your risks**

- [ ] Complete a **written risk assessment**: what customer information you hold, where it lives, and what could threaten it.
- [ ] Keep an **inventory** of the systems and data involved.

**Safeguards**

- [ ] **Access controls**: staff can reach only the customer information they need.
- [ ] **Encryption** of customer information in transit and at rest.
- [ ] **Multi-factor authentication** for anyone accessing your information systems.
- [ ] **Secure disposal** of customer information you no longer need.
- [ ] **Change management**: think about security before changing systems.
- [ ] **Monitoring and logging** of what authorized users do.

**Testing**

- [ ] Either **continuous monitoring**, or **annual penetration testing plus vulnerability assessments** every six months.

**Vendors and incidents**

- [ ] **Oversee service providers**: pick vendors who can protect the data, and require it in your contracts.
- [ ] Write an **incident response plan**.
- [ ] Know the **notification requirement**: certain security events involving the information of 500 or more consumers must be reported to the FTC within 30 days of discovery.

## "We're too small for this"

Some requirements are relaxed for firms that hold information on fewer than 5,000 consumers, including the written risk assessment, the annual report and the written incident response plan. The safeguards themselves still apply, and writing things down is still the best evidence that you took them seriously. Your attorney can confirm exactly which relaxations apply to you.

## Where to start this month

1. **Turn on multi-factor authentication** for email and every system that holds client data. It is required, and it blocks most account-takeover attempts.
2. **Encrypt laptops** and move client files to an encrypted drive.
3. **Schedule staff training** before the next tax season, when phishing peaks.
4. **Start your written plan** with the risk assessment. An outside assessment gives you a documented starting point.
